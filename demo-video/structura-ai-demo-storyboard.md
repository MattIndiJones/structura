# STRUCTURA — demonstration video · storyboard

**Deliverable:** `structura-ai-demo.mp4` — 1920×1080, 16:9, ~110 s, no sound required.
**Audience:** investment banks, private banks, asset managers, structurers, quants.
**Producer:** TP Advisory Services.

---

## Governing rule

Everything shown from Scene 3 onwards is the real STRUCTURA application, driven
live by Playwright against the running server. The script, the price, the
Greeks, the schedule — all produced by the engine during the take. Nothing is
mocked, redrawn, or retyped afterwards.

The **only** liberty taken in the edit is shortening waits. The model needed
~166 s to write the script; the cut keeps the click and the result, not the
wait. The result on screen is the one that click produced.

Scenes 1, 2 and 8 are motion-graphic cards (`cards/*.html`) — they explain, they
do not pretend to be the product.

---

## Why this product, and not exactly the one first proposed

The brief asked for a **5Y** worst-of autocall on EURO STOXX 50 / S&P 500 /
Nikkei 225 with a **60 % European** protection barrier. The three underlyings
survive unchanged — all three exist in STRUCTURA's real catalogue
(`^STOXX50E`, `^GSPC`, `^N225`), so the product is genuine, not a mock-up.

Two things were changed, both for the same reason: **the script shown on screen
must say exactly what the typed description says.** A structurer watching this
video would catch any gap between the two, and that gap would cost more
credibility than either detail is worth.

| Brief | Filmed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 60 % **European** barrier | 60 % barrier **observed at any time** | Asked for a European barrier, the model writes `WOF_MIN` — the continuous minimum, i.e. an American barrier. STRUCTURA's own control sheet flags the mismatch. |
| **5-year** | **3-year** | The maturity handed to the model is the one configured in the Pricer, and that field becomes read-only as soon as a script defines its own observation dates. |
| **quarterly** observations | **annual** observations | Quarterly makes the model emit twelve hard-coded dates on one line — unreadable at video size. Annual gives `AT 1, 2, 3:`, the classic Athena shape. The Pricer's *Expert* mode, which would emit a `CONSTAT()` schedule, only governs which example templates load; it does not change what the assistant writes. |

Filmed product: 3Y worst-of autocall on three real catalogue indices, annual
observations, 8 % conditional coupon, autocall at 100 %, 60 % continuous
protection barrier. Generated by `qwen2.5-coder:14b`, running locally.

**Correlations.** The matrix is set to 0.60 during the take — via the
application's own parallel-shock control — and the step is **not in the cut**.
It happens *before* the generation, off camera.

That is a compromise, and worth stating. The business order is the opposite:
market assumptions get adjusted once the product is defined, not before you know
what you are pricing — which is why the step is not shown. But the assistant's
control pricing runs *during* generation, so setting correlations afterwards
meant it priced at ρ = 0, and its check line then read "ρ = 0.00" on screen.
Between showing a wrong-looking figure and filming a slightly out-of-order
setup, the second was chosen, to be revisited later.

Leaving the matrix at zero was not an option: on a worst-of, zero correlation
maximises dispersion and produces a price no quant would take seriously. The
difference is measurable — 82.77 % of nominal at ρ = 0 against 86.56 % at
ρ = 0.60, on the same script.

**Cutting rule for Scene 6.** Every marker is written *after* its tab has
rendered, never at the click. Taking the head of an interval therefore shows the
*previous* tab — the shot captioned "probabilities" was showing the cash-flow
table. All result segments are cut from the tail of their interval; only the
results panel, already on screen when pricing ends, is cut from the head.

### SCENE 2 — Architecture (card)

The flow builds one node at a time — shown all at once it reads as decoration,
built it reads as a sequence:

```
Product description / Term sheet
        ↓
AI-assisted translation          ← accent blue, the differentiator
        ↓
Executable product script
        ↓
STRUCTURA quantitative engine    ← solid green, this is what computes
        ↓
PRICING · RISK · SCENARIOS · LIFECYCLE
```

Then, in sequence:

1. **AI-assisted, ~~not AI-priced~~.**
2. *The AI translates the product specification into STRUCTURA's controlled
   quantitative building blocks. It never computes the price.*
3. **Transparent. Editable. Reproducible.**

### SCENE 3 — Product input (real app)

**The script editor is emptied first — before anything else.** It is reduced to
the two comment lines of the application's own blank state:

```
# Script libre — décrivez votre payoff.
# Aide : bouton ✨ Assistant IA, ou le mémo de vocabulaire ci-dessous.
```

The `AT MATURITY: / PAY 1` skeleton that normally follows is removed: those two
lines read as a script, and the whole point of Scene 4 is that the editor starts
empty and fills up. A comments-only script still parses cleanly (checked:
`ok: true`, no errors), so no red banner appears.

The order matters too. Blanking *after* configuring the underlyings left the
default "Autocall Athena" example on screen for about a second while passing
through the Script tab — and one second is enough to make a viewer believe a
script already existed.

Only then are the three indices selected from the real catalogue, the assistant
opened, and the description typed at reading speed:

> Create a 5-year worst-of autocall on EURO STOXX 50, S&P 500 and Nikkei 225,
> with quarterly observations, an 8% annual conditional coupon, autocall at 100%
> of the initial level, and a 60% protection barrier observed at any time.

The engine selector (Ollama, local) is framed for ~1.5 s.

**Captions:** `Describe the product in financial terms.` ·
`The model runs locally — the description never leaves the desk.`

### SCENE 4 — AI → executable script (real app)

## Deliberate omissions

- **Clients, Booking, RFQ, AMC** — real counterparty and client data. Never filmed.
- **The built-in Demo Mode** (`stores/demoMode.js`) was *not* used: it masks
  prices and blurs the script (`••••••`). It exists for screenshots where the
  numbers must be hidden — the opposite need to this video's.
- **Voice-over** — none recorded; see `structura-ai-demo-voiceover.txt`.
- **Music** — none. Silence reads as institutional; a stock corporate loop does not.

## Known limitations

**1. The interface is in French.** A project rule: UI always French. All
narrative text, captions and cards are in English, so a viewer sees an English
story over a French product. If the international pitch requires an English UI,
that is a product decision, not an editing one.

**2. Greeks are not shown.** Deliberate. Bump-and-reprice on three underlyings
runs for minutes and leaves a progress bar in the header across every
subsequent shot. Showing an engine mid-computation reads worse than not showing
it, so the risk claim rests on the architecture card and on the Greeks selector
visible in Scene 3. A separate take, waiting for the computation, would be the
way to show them properly.

**3. The price is a demonstration, not a quote.** Volatilities come from the
application's own calibration on 243 observations; correlations are set to a
flat 0.60 rather than pair-estimated. Everything on screen is what the engine
computed from those inputs — but the inputs are a demo configuration.

**4. Four statistics are blurred, on purpose.** The control sheet's tiles —
autocall probability, barrier touch, capital loss, expected life — came out with
autocall, barrier touch and capital loss at the *same* value. That does not hold
up, and it is a product question to settle, not an editing one. Until it is,
those numbers stay out of a commercial video: the region is blurred in the two
shots that show it (`TUILES` in `assemble.mjs`). The rest of the control sheet —
the five checks, including the control pricing — stays legible.

**This is a real finding worth chasing**: the same three probabilities landing
on one value points at a shared code path in the control-pricing statistics, not
at a coincidence.
